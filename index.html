Project Showcase

Redesign of PExA Instrument

Product Development Medical Tech

Designed to help identify biomarkers in respiratory diseases, the PExA 2.1 is a non invasive medical device utilized by research centers and pharmaceutical companies. This redesign project involved optimizing the internal volume and layout, implementing a manually adjusted lead screw, and restructuring the air storage into a cylinder shape. We developed physical prototypes to ensure an intuitive and minimalist design, focusing on ergonomics for patients, operators, and service personnel.

B.Sc. Thesis: Sensorized Exoskeleton

Robotics Unity

To combat patient demotivation during knee rehabilitation, we developed a lightweight, 3D printed sensorized exoskeleton. Equipped with an angular encoder, IMU, and EMG sensors, the device collects real time kinematic data processed via micro ROS and ROS 2. This data directly controls a game application in Unity, successfully gamifying the physiotherapy process and ensuring accurate exercise adherence.

Modular Coffee Machine

Machine Design LCA & FMEA

Developed a shared product architecture to serve two distinct market segments: a budget friendly autonomous brewer and a premium sustainable brewer. We performed comprehensive Life Cycle Assessments and Failure Mode and Effects Analysis, selecting eco friendly materials like oak and recycled plastics to significantly lower the environmental impact while achieving a robust, modular design optimized for assembly.

Additive Manufacturing: Steering Knuckle

Additive Manufacturing Topology Optimization

Engineered a high performance steering knuckle for a miniature terrain buggy, leveraging additive manufacturing to achieve an optimized geometry. Using Fusion 360's generative design and topology optimization, unnecessary mass was removed to reduce unsprung weight, utilizing 4130 steel powder to guarantee strength and toughness under dynamic loads.

Machine Design (7.5 hp)

In the Machine Design course, we applied systematic engineering tools to analyze and redesign a consumer coffee maker. The project focused on optimizing the mechanical architecture by utilizing Design for Assembly (DFA) principles to streamline production and reduce complexity. To ensure product reliability and ecological sustainability, we conducted a Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) alongside a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA). Working collaboratively, we evaluated material selections to minimize environmental impact and presented our final optimized design through CAD models and technical reports.
